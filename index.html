## Atmospheric Reality Gap

Your hero sequence opens with a lab shot that has genuine energy — warm amber light raking across two researchers bent over instrumentation, shallow depth of field pulling focus cleanly, safety glasses signaling credibility without staging it. That image earns attention. Then the sequence collapses. Frame two is a headless-suits-reviewing-charts stock photo: no faces, no location, no company signal, indistinguishable from a mid-tier accounting firm's slide deck. A buyer evaluating a €7.3 billion testing network is now looking at imagery that could belong to any vendor on their shortlist. The Nantes facility exterior — shot at blue hour with warm interior light glowing through the windows — partially recovers the thread, but the tonal palette (cool blue lab, flat white business shot, dusk blue exterior) transmits visual incoherence, not precision science leadership. Your copy promises "innovative and high-quality laboratory services." The business-meeting stock image signals neither. That mismatch is where high-value procurement contacts quietly move their browser tab elsewhere.

## Visual Narrative Coherence

The most expensive fracture in your visual journey is `screenshot-2026-04-01-115939.jpg` — a filename that announces to every image crawler, and to any buyer who inspects page source, that a screenshot was grabbed and uploaded as live homepage content. It sits in the "Our impact and innovation" zone, which is exactly where enterprise buyers are forming vendor confidence. That asset has no descriptive alt text, and its filename provides zero subject context. It reads as operational neglect at the exact moment the page is trying to claim scientific rigor.

Supporting this: your "Our impact and innovation" grid links to nine anchor targets on the same page — images named `4.jpg`, `5.jpg`, `7.jpg`, `8.jpg`, `10.jpg`, `26.png`. Numbered filenames with no subject descriptors mean the visual story of your innovation content cannot be read by search infrastructure or screen readers. A procurement officer at a pharma company running accessibility audits on vendor sites flags this. That is a lost enterprise inquiry, not a UX note.

## Metadata and Conversion Gaps

Your 0 broken links and #1 organic rank for "Eurofins Enviroment Testing" confirm the technical foundation is solid. The findability damage is concentrated inside your image layer. Of 46 content images on the homepage, 5 — 11% — carry no alt text. That is not a compliance rounding error at your scale; it means 11% of your visual content is invisible to search indexing and inaccessible to assistive technology users, including clinical and regulatory professionals who frequently use screen readers.

The filenames compound the loss. `agroscience-services_6189-x-3481-px.jpg` embeds pixel dimensions as the primary descriptor — Google reads "6189 x 3481 px," not "agroscience testing laboratory." `business-team-in-a-small-meeting-in-the-office_9000-x-6000-px.jpg` does the same. These are dimension stamps from a photo library, not semantic signals. Combined with `screenshot-2026-04-01-115939.jpg` carrying neither alt text nor a descriptive name, you are forfeiting image search visibility across a 46-image homepage for a company running 450 million tests per year. Your og:image is present and your meta description hits 160 characters — the page-level infrastructure is correct. The image-level infrastructure is transferring ranking equity to competitors whose assets are properly labeled.

Your page title at 65 characters is within range, but it leads with "Eurofins" — the brand name already held at position #1 — rather than the testing category terms that capture net-new buyers. That is a missed conversion opportunity built into the first thing Google displays.
